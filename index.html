PROJECT NOTES / 01Bangkok Road Accident Analysis+

Problem

How can reported accident records be organized to explore patterns over time and communicate road safety questions?

Data & scope

Open data from Thailand’s Ministry of Transport, focused on Bangkok. Expanded from the original 2022–2025 university course project to cover 2019–2025. This is not a complete census of accidents on every Bangkok road.

Visit the data source ↗

Cleaning & analysis

The original project used Excel and Power BI. The updated version uses Python/Pandas for data cleaning and standardization across years, alongside an updated Power BI dashboard. The original course project also included exploratory time-series forecasting with Prophet.

Insight & interpretation

The project highlights the importance of separating recorded accident counts from risk: counts alone do not account for traffic exposure. Comparisons across years also depend on coverage and completeness.

Interpretation guidelines

Compare reporting periods and category definitions before interpreting trends. Use the dashboard to explore recorded patterns; assessing relative road risk requires additional traffic exposure data.

My contribution

I carried out the original course project and independently developed this updated version, expanding the time coverage and improving data preparation and visualization.

EARLIER COURSE PROJECT · 2022–2025 DATA

Exploring time-series forecasting with Prophet

I experimented with Prophet in Python to produce a 365-day forecast and explore trend, weekly, and yearly components. These figures come from the earlier university project, rather than a new model trained on the expanded 2019–2025 dataset.

Experimental work: These plots illustrate model outputs, not demonstrated forecasting accuracy. No holdout evaluation or baseline comparison is presented here. Seasonal components describe estimated contributions to the forecast, not observed accident counts or evidence that holidays cause accidents.

Further validation: Use a chronological holdout or rolling evaluation and compare with a simple seasonal baseline before using forecasts to support decisions.

PROJECT NOTES / 04Healthy Lifestyle in Bangkok+

Question

What does access to exercise facilities and green spaces mean for everyday life in Bangkok?

Research approach

Synthesized published statistics, articles, and reports into an illustrated academic feature. This work uses secondary sources rather than an original field survey.

Story & policy context

Connected differences in public-space access with urban wellbeing and discussed initiatives such as the 15-minute park concept and the reuse of vacant land.

What I learned

How a place is classified matters: a green area is not necessarily a publicly accessible park. Clear definitions, source attribution, and careful visual communication are essential when telling a story with data.
